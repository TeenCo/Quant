# Nationalism and Economic Exchange: Evidence from Shocks to Sino-Japanese Relations

## Bibliography

- **Authors:** Raymond Fisman, Yasushi Hamao, Yongxiang Wang
- **Date / version:** NBER Working Paper 20089, May 2014; published in *Review of Financial Studies* 27(9), 2014, pp. 2626–2660.
- **DOI:** [10.3386/w20089](https://doi.org/10.3386/w20089)
- **Primary record:** [NBER Working Paper 20089](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20089)

## Assessment

- **Evidence grade:** 2 — peer-reviewed event-study evidence on international political/economic exposure; event windows and sample are specific.
- **Recency:** 2014; mechanism remains relevant, but institutional and geopolitical contexts differ.
- **Novelty:** Uses firm-level exposure to a rarely quantified political friction channel.
- **Source type:** Peer-reviewed corporate finance / international economics study.

## Summary

The authors examine adverse Sino-Japanese relation shocks in 2005 and 2010. Firms with greater exposure to the other country experienced relative stock-price declines around the events. Heterogeneity by state-owned-enterprise competition and employment exposure provides evidence that institutional and economic links mediate effects.

## Research use and limitations

- This is a useful template for mapping geopolitical events to cross-sectional firm exposure, not a reusable guarantee that political news creates a predictable edge.
- Event timing, anticipation, concurrent news, exposure measurement, and multiple-event selection require scrutiny.
- Candidate replication: build a point-in-time exposure map, predefine the event window and controls, test placebos, and evaluate pre-event drift and executable post-release returns.
