# The Natural Rate Puzzle: Global Macro Trends and the Market-Implied r*

## Bibliography

- **Authors:** Josh Davis, Cristian Fuenzalida, Alan M. Taylor
- **Date / version:** NBER Working Paper 26560, December 2019; revised January 2021.
- **DOI:** [10.3386/w26560](https://doi.org/10.3386/w26560)
- **Primary source:** [NBER Working Paper 26560](https://www.nber.org/papers/w26560)

## Assessment

- **Evidence grade:** 2 — academic macro-finance research with explicit model comparison and international scope; model estimates remain assumption-dependent and are not direct tradable observations.
- **Recency:** 2019/2021 revision; rates regimes and macro estimates continue to evolve.
- **Novelty:** Connects a market-implied natural-rate estimate, inflation expectations, and bond risk premia in a unified macro-finance framework.
- **Source type:** NBER working paper.

## Summary

The authors identify inconsistencies between benchmark macroeconomic estimates of the natural rate and finance-model estimates of bond risk premia. Their unified model uses long-run trend factors and reports improved explanatory power for yields/returns. They also report trading strategies based on combined natural-rate and inflation trends outperforming yield-only strategies in the studied sample.

## Research use and limitations

- `r*` is latent and model-dependent, not directly observed. Distinguish real-time estimates from revised or full-sample smoothed estimates.
- The reported strategy performance needs independent replication, realistic costs, point-in-time factor estimates, and multiple-testing context.
- Useful for macro-finance feature ideas and consistency checks across yield, inflation, and macro models.
