# Mathematics for Quantitative Research

Mathematics supplies the language for modeling uncertainty, extracting structure from high-dimensional data, estimating parameters, and solving constrained decision problems. The practical goal is to understand assumptions and failure modes, not only to manipulate formulas.

## Core branches

- [[Linear algebra]] — vectors, matrices, projections, eigenstructure, decompositions, conditioning.
- [[Calculus and optimization]] — derivatives, gradients, constraints, convexity, numerical optimization.
- [[Probability]] — conditional probability, random variables, dependence, tails, stochastic processes.
- [[Statistics]] — estimation, hypothesis tests, intervals, resampling, robust inference.
- [[Topology and geometry]] — metric spaces, manifolds, neighborhoods, curvature, dimensionality, persistence.
- [[Econometrics]] — estimation under dependence and empirical identification.

## Applications

Portfolio construction and factor models use linear algebra; likelihood and gradient-based learning use calculus; risk and uncertainty use probability/statistics; option prices and time-varying systems use stochastic processes; high-dimensional signals and nonlinear embeddings motivate geometry/topology. All applications inherit data and validation constraints in [[Research workflow]].

## Learning sequence

1. Single/multivariable calculus and linear algebra fundamentals.
2. Probability distributions, conditional expectation, dependence, and limit theorems.
3. Statistical inference and regression; time-series dependence and econometrics.
4. Convex optimization, numerical methods, and computational linear algebra.
5. Stochastic calculus, advanced time series, and geometric/topological methods as research questions require.
